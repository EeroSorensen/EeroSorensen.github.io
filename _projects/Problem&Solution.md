## Overview ##

This project presents the design and purpoe of an app focused on improving aerobic capacity in individuals with Down syndrome. The app uses evidence based exercise stategies to provide practical tools and goals to support physical activity. Emphasizing user-friendly navigation it tailors engaging content for indviduals to interact with. Drawing on principles used in literature, it demonstrates how structured, enjoyable and supportive resources can promote long-term health, independence and quality of life.

---

## The Problem ## 
Aerobic capacity is the body's ability to take in, transport and use oxygen during sustained exercise. Sedentary lifestyles reduce aerobic capacity by weakening cardivascular function, reducing muscle oxidative capacity, and limiting oxygen delivery.

This is of critical concern for those with Down syndrome, who face both physiological and environmental barriers to physical activity: 
Physiological: Muscle hypotonia, ligament laxity, congenital heart disease risk, and low overall fitness

Environmental: Lack of support systems, limited access to specialized programs, logistical barriers

Psychological: Low motivation, cognitive limitations, fear and anxiety around exercise

The data tells a striking story:

Statistic | Value
------------- | -------------
Adults with DS meeting activity guidelines | <10% (Mendonca et al., 2010)
Affected by high sedentary behaviour | 79% (Mendonca et al., 2010)
Youth not meeting 90 min/week threshold | ~58% (Melo et al., 2022)
V̇O₂max of young adult with DS vs. typical adult | Same as a 60-year-old without DS (Gawlik et al., 2016)
Peak VO2 in adolescents with DS vs. typical | 26 mL/kg/min vs. 50–52 mL/kg/min (Mendonca et al., 2010)

## The Solution ##
A free, gamified mobile application that connects to a smartwatch or step tracker to promote physical activity in adolescents with Down syndrome.

As users increase their steps or active minutes, they are rewarded through a gamified system — a digital plant grows or a virtual pet becomes happier and more energetic. This creates an engaging, motivating experience with clear visual feedback on progress.

---

## How It Works ##
1. The app operates through a simple three-step loop:

2. Track Movement — The app connects to a smartwatch or step tracker and monitors daily steps and active minutes in real time

3. Reward Activity — Movement is converted into rewards: a set number of steps or active minutes causes the digital plant to grow or the virtual pet to improve in energy and happiness

Reinforce Behaviour — Positive feedback (sounds, animations, praise messages) encourages consistency and habit formation over time

This loop is designed to be low-barrier and immediately rewarding — making physical activity feel accessible and fun rather than effortful.

## Intervention Summary ##
Category | Description
------------- | -------------
Population | Adolescents with Down syndrome who may require caregiver support
Goal | Increase daily physical activity by making movement fun, simple, and rewarding
Primary Outcome | Improved physical activity levels and aerobic capacity
Approach | Gamification + tracking + visual feedback
Support System | Caregiver involvement to guide, monitor, and encourage

## Expected Impact ##
Increased daily step counts and active minutes

* Improved V̇O₂max and cardiorespiratory fitness over time

* Reduced risk of cardiovascular disease and obesity

* Greater independence and community participation

* Improved mental health outcomes through consistent movement and social engagement
